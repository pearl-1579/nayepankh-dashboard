Organizational Timeline
2020 — COVID-19 Pandemic
Origin: Grassroots Response
A group of high school students started distributing food and essentials to underprivileged families during India's lockdown, with whatever resources they could gather.
March 28, 2021 — Official Launch
NayePankh Foundation Registered
Formally registered under the Indian Society Act, 1860. Acquired the name "NayePankh" (New Wings) symbolizing upliftment of the underprivileged.
Aug 2022 — Digital Expansion
12A & 80G + YouTube Launch + NITI Aayog
Received government tax certifications (donors get 50% income tax relief). Launched YouTube channel. Achieved NITI Aayog affiliation. Expanded to Ghaziabad.
2023 — 1 Lakh Milestone
100,000+ People Helped
Media coverage in Dainik Jagran, Pioneer, and Hindustan. Instagram community crossed 10K followers. Sanitary napkin campaigns expanded statewide.
2024–2025 — National Scale
30,000+ Interns · 2 Lakh+ Beneficiaries
Became one of India's largest student-led NGOs. 36+ internship domains listed on Internshala. Technical Internship Program launched with 16+ roles.
2026 — Present
Continued Mission: "Badalte Bharat Ki Nayi Tasveer"
Ongoing programs in food distribution, menstrual hygiene, education, and stray animal care. Social media at 30K+ verified followers. Expanding digital outreach and corporate partnerships.

Analyst Insights

🚀 Rapid Grassroots Scale

NayePankh grew from a handful of high schoolers to 30,000+ interns in just 5 years — a compound annual growth rate of ~150% in volunteer/intern count. This reflects the power of the youth-led model and fully remote internship structure.

🍱 Food Distribution Leads Impact

~42% of total beneficiary touchpoints come from food distribution, making it the flagship program. Maintaining and scaling this program should remain the top operational priority.

📱 Instagram Dominates · YouTube Untapped

Instagram (16.4K, 284 posts) is the core channel. YouTube has 5.05K subscribers but only 3 videos — each averaging 2,630 views. Consistent uploads could make it the foundation's biggest reach multiplier.

🎓 Internship = Force Multiplier

30,000+ interns don't just operate the org — each intern amplifies outreach through personal networks, effectively turning every intern into a micro-ambassador. This is the model's biggest hidden asset.

📅 Seasonal Peaks: Winter & Festivals

Activity peaks in October–January (Diwali, winter drives) and March–April (anniversary + summer food drives). Planning resources and intern recruitment should align to these cycles.

🩸 Hygiene Program: High Impact, Low Visibility

45K+ women reached but the menstrual hygiene program gets far less social spotlight than food drives. Greater visibility here could unlock dedicated CSR funding and health-focused partnerships.

SWOT Analysis — NayePankh Foundation

Strategic assessment based on verified research data
💪 Strengths
  • Youth-led, highly relatable model
  • UP Govt. registered + NITI Aayog affiliated
  • 80G/12A — donors get 50% tax relief
  • 30K+ intern network as force multiplier
  • Multi-program diversity across 5+ areas
  • 16+ Technical internship roles available
⚠️ Weaknesses
  • Primarily Kanpur/Ghaziabad geography
  • High volunteer turnover (student-based)
  • No dedicated data tracking system
  • YouTube severely underutilized (3 videos)
  • Twitter/X very low reach (791 followers)
🌱 Opportunities
  • CSR partnerships with corporates
  • Pan-India expansion via remote model
  • Government welfare scheme collaboration
  • YouTube content strategy — big sub base
  • Data-driven impact reporting for donors
🔴 Threats
  • Competition from established NGOs for funding
  • Economic fluctuations affecting donations
  • Intern quality variability at scale
  • Social media algorithm changes
  • Regulatory compliance complexity
